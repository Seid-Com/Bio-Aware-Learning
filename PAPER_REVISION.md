## 5. Sections 5.2–5.4 — aggregation mathematics (fixes reviewer note on

Replace equations (2)–(4) and their surrounding definitions with:

> **5.2 Proposed Bio-Aware Trust-Weighted Aggregation**
>
> w^{t+1} = Σ_{i=1}^{N} γ_i · w_i^t,  (2)
>
> where the combined aggregation weight is the renormalized product of the
> bio-aware weight and the blockchain trust score:
>
> γ_i = (α_i · β_i) / (Σ_{j=1}^{N} α_j · β_j),   Σ_i γ_i = 1.
>
> **5.3 Bio-Aware Weight α_i**
>
> The bio-aware weight is a temperature softmax over a composite score that
> *includes the sample-size (FedAvg) term*, biological similarity, and update
> quality:
>
> α_i = exp(r_i / τ) / Σ_j exp(r_j / τ),   (3)
>
> r_i = μ_b · b_i + μ_s · s_i + μ_q · q_i,   with (μ_b, μ_s, μ_q) = (0.4, 0.3, 0.3), τ = 1,
>
> where:
> - s_i = n_i / Σ_j n_j is the standard FedAvg sample-proportion term;
> - b_i = mean_j [ 0.6 · cos(bf_i, bf_j) + 0.4 · C(omics_i, omics_j) ] is the
>   average biological similarity of client i to all peers, combining cosine
>   similarity of multi-omics pathway signatures with a domain-knowledge
>   omics-compatibility matrix C;
> - q_i = 0.4 · lossScore_i + 0.4 · accScore_i + 0.2 · gradConsistency_i is
>   the update-quality score (min–max normalized local loss and accuracy, plus
>   a consistency term that favors gradient norms near the cohort median and
>   penalizes outliers).
>
> Because the sample proportion s_i enters r_i before normalization, the
> resulting weights remain strictly monotone in s_i, so the sample-size
> ordering of Eq. (1) is preserved even when μ_b = μ_q = 0 (the softmax

> trigger escalating penalties; honest clients accrue toward 1.0; flagged
> clients decay toward a floor of 0.05). Normalization of β_i is implicit in
> γ_i, which guarantees Σ_i γ_i = 1. Consequently,
>
> lim_{β_i → 0} γ_i = 0,
>
> i.e., a client whose trust vanishes has vanishing influence on the global
> model.

## 7. Section 8 — complete replacement

### 8.1 Datasets

> All reported experiments are conducted on a fully synthetic, seeded,
> multi-omics healthcare benchmark so that every number in this paper is
> reproducible from source without data-access constraints. Ten federated
> clients each hold 500 ± 100 samples from one of five omics domains—genomic
> (128 raw features), proteomic (96), transcriptomic (112), epigenomic (80),
> and clinical (32). Labels are drawn from an omics-dependent class prior
> (skew ≤ 0.2 under the non-IID "mild" partition), and features follow
> label-conditional Gaussian distributions whose means shift by class and
> whose variance grows with the omics domain. Raw features are standardized
> and projected to a shared 64-dimensional representation by a fixed random
> projection, after which a linear logistic model with binary cross-entropy
> loss and L2 regularization (λ = 0.01) is trained locally.
>
> This benchmark captures the heterogeneity properties relevant to the paper's
> claims—non-IID label shift, covariate shift across institutions, and
> modality heterogeneity—while removing confounds from data-access and
> preprocessing variability. Validation on real clinical cohorts (TCGA
> multi-omics and MIMIC-III EHRs, with log-transformation/z-score
> normalization and median/one-hot imputation respectively) is deferred to
> future work (Section 10); results on such cohorts must not be inferred from
> the present study.

### 8.2 Baseline Methods

> The proposed framework is evaluated against three ablation baselines that
> isolate its components, all implemented in the same codebase and executed
> under identical seeds, data partitions, and training schedules:
>
> 1. **FedAvg** — standard federated averaging (sample-proportional weights),
>    without bio-awareness, trust, DP, or secure aggregation.
> 2. **Bio-Aware FL** — biological-similarity weighting only (no trust layer).
> 3. **Trust-Aware FL** — blockchain trust scoring only (no bio-awareness).
>
> Full TA-BC-BFL enables all components (bio-aware + trust + differential
> privacy + homomorphic secure aggregation). Additional blockchain-coordination
> baselines (e.g., Blockchain-FL, Trust-FL) are implemented in the simulation
> framework but are not part of the reported quantitative matrix.

### 8.3 Experimental Setup

> Experiments run on commodity CPU hardware (no GPU required) with Python
> 3.12 and NumPy/SciPy. Training uses plain mini-batch SGD (learning rate
> 0.001, batch size 64, 5 local epochs per round, L2 = 0.01) for 100 global
> communication rounds with full client participation (10 clients).
>
> Privacy is provided by central differential privacy at the server: the
> aggregated update is released with Gaussian noise calibrated as
> σ = √(2 ln(1.25/δ))/ε, δ = 10⁻⁵, clipping norm C = 1.0, and sensitivity
> S = 2C/n over the n aggregated samples, with advanced-composition
> accounting; ε = 1.0 in all experiments except the ε-sweep. Homomorphic
> secure aggregation is simulated as a fixed-point quantized partially
> homomorphic scheme (2048-bit key parameter) that decrypts only the weighted
> aggregate.
>
> The adversary corrupts a fraction f = 0.2 of clients with poison rate
> p = 0.3: (i) model poisoning adds zero-mean Gaussian noise of σ = 3.0 + 5p
> = 4.5 to the outgoing weights each round; (ii) label flipping permanently
> inverts 30% of an adversarial client's training labels; (iii) Sybil adds
> Gaussian perturbations of σ = 2.0 + 4p = 3.2 to outgoing weights each round.
>
> Every condition is repeated under five seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1024),
> paired across methods: for a given seed all methods see identical client
> data, sample counts, and initialization. Reported values are mean ± standard
> deviation; comparisons use two-sided paired t-tests (α = 0.05).

### 8.4 Metrics

> Four metrics are evaluated quantitatively: (i) **accuracy** — final-round
> global test accuracy; (ii) **attack resistance** — accuracy retained under
> each attack, reported both absolutely and as the drop from the method's own
> clean accuracy; (iii) **privacy** — the ε/accuracy trade-off together with
> gradient-inversion reconstruction quality (1 = full leakage, measured
> against the clean pre-noise gradient, with a no-DP baseline); and
> (iv) **scalability** — wall-clock seconds per communication round as a
> function of client count.

> 87.0% ± 4.0 and Full TA-BC-BFL 88.5% ± 3.2 (95% CI [84.5, 92.5]). The
> difference between Full TA-BC-BFL and FedAvg is not statistically
> significant (p = 0.062): the trust layer imposes a modest, non-significant
> clean-accuracy cost, and bio-aware weighting is accuracy-neutral on this
> benchmark.
>

### 8.8 Discussion

> The evaluation supports one central claim and qualifies another. First, the
> blockchain trust layer converts the catastrophic poisoning and Sybil
> failures of FedAvg into moderate degradations, yielding statistically
> significant +15.0- and +20.8-point advantages at ε = 1.0 with secure
> aggregation enabled; the ablation attributes this effect specifically to
> trust weighting. Second, TA-BC-BFL does not improve clean accuracy on this
> benchmark—its clean performance is statistically on par with FedAvg
> (p = 0.062)—so the framework's empirical justification is robustness, not
> predictive superiority. Third, bio-aware weighting is accuracy-neutral
> without genuine pathway structure; its benefit can only be established on
> curated multi-omics cohorts where pathway coherence interacts with the
> prediction task. Practitioners should deploy TA-BC-BFL when poisoning or
> Sybil participants are plausible and prefer plain FedAvg when all
> participants are trusted and clean accuracy alone is the objective.

## 10. Appendix — new Proposition 1 (A.2) and influence instrumentation

**Appendix.docx, new §A.2 "Proposition 1: Bounded Influence of Detected
Adversarial Clients".** Statement: with combined weights
γ_i = α_iβ_i / Σ_j α_jβ_j, adversarial bio-aware share s, adversarial trust
≤ β_A and honest trust ≥ β_H in a round,

> W_adv = Σ_{i∈Adv} γ_i ≤ s·β_A / (s·β_A + (1 − s)·β_H)

proved by N ≤ β_A·s, U ≥ β_H·(1 − s) and monotonicity of x ↦ x/(x + U).
Corollary with deployed parameters (s ≈ 0.2, trust floor 0.05, unflagged
honest trust ≥ 0.7): W_adv ≤ 1.75%, tightening to ≈ 1.23% as honest trust
converges to 1.0 — versus an unattenuated 20%. Measured trajectory: flagged
adversaries reach the floor by round 5; adversarial aggregation weight falls
16.8% (round 1) → 1.3% (round 5) → 1.1% (steady state), always ≤ the
per-round bound.

**Tie-in added to §8.5 of the revised paper** (after "…within about 15
rounds."): *"This attenuation is quantified analytically by the influence
bound of Proposition 1 (Appendix A.2)."*
